# Creator/Influencer Marketing for Roblox Games and Launch-Week + First-28-Days Tactics (as of 1 Oct 2026)

Scope note: Notes cover (1) working with YouTubers/TikTokers in the Roblox anime niche, (2) the promo-code ecosystem, (3) launch mechanics, (4) the first 28 days, (5) risks. Each figure is labelled **[official]** (Roblox docs/announcements), **[community]** (DevForum/creator reports), or **[estimate]** (agencies, rate surveys, third-party analysts). Items older than 2025 are flagged **[older]**. Environment constraint: devforum.roblox.com, create.roblox.com, about.roblox.com, help.roblox.com, Wikipedia and most gaming sites were blocked for full-page fetch; those are cited from search-result summaries and from the open-source docs mirror (raw.githubusercontent.com/Roblox/creator-docs), which was readable in full.

User-context quick verdicts (details and sources in the sections below):
- "~₪300 (~$80) for small creators" → feasible only at the nano tier (1K–10K subs) or as Robux/early-access barter; ask for Shorts/TikToks rather than long-form. [estimate]
- "Concentrated 72-hour burst" → partially supported. Official docs say discovery signals are per-user averages measured over day 1 / days 2–7 / days 8–28, so a burst helps only if the players it brings retain; a burst of low-retention traffic is actively penalised. A burst is still the right way to get the first *qualified* play sessions and to make launch-week creator content coincide. [official + inference]
- "Promo codes incl. creator codes" → supported; standard practice in anime games (RELEASE / UPDATE1 / 100KLIKES / creator-name codes). Code-listing sites pick codes up from the game's Discord/X within hours to days. [community/observed]
- "Weekly update tags in the title" → no official rule found that bans "[UPDATE]"/"[NEW RACE]" tags, but official docs say hashtags/tags are ignored by ranking, and multiple developers report search-rank drops after title edits. Put update tags in the description's "Recent Updates" section and in the thumbnail, keep the title stable. [official + community]
- "Prepare week-2 update before launch" → supported by the 28-day retention window and by how Grow a Garden (weekly events) and Blox Fruits (Patch 13) actually grew. [official + press]
- Missing from the plan: (a) a possibly mandatory refundable 1,000-Robux publishing fee / subscription requirement for all-ages distribution [official, new in 2026 docs]; (b) share links per creator for attribution [official]; (c) Experience Events + update announcements as free notification channels [official]; (d) the "like-for-reward" prohibition and the 16+ gate on Discord links [official/community]; (e) the "Blox"-in-title ban and anime-IP DMCA history [official/press].

## Key Question 1: Creator marketing — prices by tier, barter deals, finding/contacting creators, official programs, conversion reports, creator codes, disclosure rules

### Takeaway
No Roblox-specific audited rate card exists; agency and rate-survey estimates converge on roughly $20–100 per video for 1K–10K-sub Roblox YouTubers, $100–300 for 10K–50K, $200–1,000 for 50K–200K, and $500–25,000 for dedicated videos from large channels, with TikTok nano/micro creators at $25–200 / $200–1,500 per video and Roblox creators reportedly charging less than other gaming niches. Community reports show creator videos reliably produce a short (≈48-hour) spike, but no reliable views-to-visits ratio was found. Roblox's official tooling for attribution is share links (one per creator) plus referral/launch data; disclosure is required by Roblox Advertising Standards ("ad", "paid", "sponsored") and by the FTC.

**Creator-specific codes and attribution tooling**
- Creator-named codes are an established pattern: in YouTube Life, code "Carbon" was "added for content creator CarbonMeister" and grants a statue of the creator. [community wiki] — [YouTube Life wiki](https://youtube-life-roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Codes)
- Official share links: "unique and trackable links that generate metrics that allow you to track and grow your off-platform user acquisition"; no limit on quantity; "creating different links for different channels" shows "which off-platform channel has brought in the most engaged users"; custom LaunchData via Player:GetJoinData() can give "in-game perks to users that join through your share link". Requires owner or "Create and configure share links" permission. [official] — [share-links.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/promotion/share-links.md)
- Official referral system: ReferredByPlayerId in GetJoinData() "automatically populates for all types of invitations"; developers can set a reward limit (e.g. 3 rewards per inviter), one-time rewards, and cooldowns; only one referral banner can be live at a time. [official] — [referral-system.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/promotion/referral-system.md)

**Roblox and legal rules on paid promotion / disclosure**
- Roblox Advertising Standards (introduced 2023, revised again in 2026): advertising content outside Roblox-served ad units "must be accompanied by simple and clear disclosures", with acceptable language such as "ad", "paid" or "sponsored", placed "in or near the advertising content"; ads "must not make deceptive or unrealistic claims … including offering free Robux to entice engagement"; developers are "fully responsible" for compliance. [official] — [Advertising Standards](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/13722260778260-Advertising-Standards); [DevForum New Advertising Policies & Standards (2026)](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-advertising-policies-standards/4527365); open-source doc: "If you believe your content is advertising, you must clearly disclose it as advertising in a way that Roblox users will understand in their language." — [comply-with-advertising-standards.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/promotion/comply-with-advertising-standards.md)
- FTC (US) for YouTube/TikTok creators: disclose any material connection; verbal disclosure within the first 30 seconds or at the start of the sponsored segment, written disclosure in the first lines of the description, and YouTube's "includes paid promotion" toggle, which "does not replace the FTC's verbal and written disclosure requirements"; the FTC's 2023 revision explicitly covers paying a gamer to stream a product. [legal guidance, 2026] — [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/ftc-endorsement-compliance-streamers-2026/); [YTCalculators](https://ytcalculators.com/blog/ftc-disclosure-youtube-sponsorships/); fine figure "$51K per undisclosed post" claimed by [AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/content-creator-ftc-disclosure-compliance-2026)
- 2027 change that does NOT hit this plan: from 1 Jan 2027 Roblox will charge experience owners a CPM-based fee on *brand integrations inside experiences* (brands paying developers): $1.50 CPM for US visits, $0.75 UK/AU/NZ/CA/Nordics, $0.20 Japan/W. Europe/S. Korea, $0.05 elsewhere, flat $0.10 after 28 days, forecast on the prior 56 days. Announced March 2026; KreekCraft called it "insanely anti-creator". [official via press] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/roblox-sets-cpm-based-fees-for-creator-brand-deals-starting-in-2027/); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/roblox-reveals-creator-fee-structure-for-brand-integrations-from-2027/); [KreekCraft on X](https://x.com/KreekCraft/status/2035082367983858136)

### Inferences
- With ~$80 (₪300) the realistic creator buys are: 2–4 nano YouTubers/TikTokers at $20–40 each for a Short/TikTok, or several barter deals (early access, a creator-named code, an exclusive cosmetic/statue, Robux via a group payout). A single 10K–50K channel's long-form video ($100–300) would consume the whole budget.
- Prioritise creators whose channel is already Roblox-anime-focused (community reports say conversion is best when the creator "focuses on Roblox content"); use the iqfluence/Collabstr/Social Seed lists plus the discord.gg/robloxYT and RoDevs servers to source them; search YouTube for recent "new anime game roblox" uploads under 20K views and DM those channels.
- Attribution: give each creator (a) a share link (official analytics on the "which off-platform channel" question) and (b) a creator-named code, so you get both Roblox-side acquisition data and a redeem count. Since Audience Expansion rewards pay you 35% of the first $100 spent by brand-new Roblox users who arrive via your share link, creator videos aimed at non-Roblox audiences (e.g. anime fans on TikTok) have an extra payoff — but only if your game later sustains 100+ DAU.
- Expect a creator spike to decay within ~48 hours; schedule creator posts to coincide with launch so the spike contributes to day-1/day-2–7 retention signals rather than arriving as isolated low-retention traffic.
- Ask every paid or gifted creator to label the video "sponsored"/"paid promotion"; Roblox's standards and the FTC both require it, and the reputational cost of an undisclosed deal in the kid-heavy Roblox community is high.

### Gaps
- No audited or large-sample Roblox-specific rate card for 1K–10K / 10K–100K / 100K–1M creators was found; all tier prices are agency or rate-survey estimates, and bloxg/Hypertube/Modash/SponsorRadar pages could not be fetched in full (domain blocked), so their figures are quoted from search summaries.
- No developer postmortem with an explicit views→visits percentage was found; only anecdotal spikes (1–4M views → +1,600 CCU for 48h). Roblox does not publish per-source conversion benchmarks.
- No documented, named cases of small creators formally accepting Robux-only or early-access-only deals were found; the practice is described by an agency guide and implied by DevForum "free promotion" threads only.
- No official Roblox program for matching small developers with creators was found (Video Stars serves creators; Creator Rewards pays developers).

## Key Question 2: Promo-code ecosystem — how codes drive traffic, how listing sites discover games, SEO, cadence, typical anime-game rewards

### Takeaway
Code-listing sites (Pro Game Guides, Try Hard Guides, Pocket Tactics, PCGamesN, Pocket Gamer, Beebom, Roblox Den, PlayPatch, RoCodes etc.) source codes from the game's own Discord/X/Roblox group within hours to days of a post and maintain weekly-verified evergreen "codes (Month 2026)" articles; anime games follow a predictable pattern of RELEASE → UPDATE1…N → like-milestone codes (100KLIKES etc.) rewarding spins/rerolls/gems, with roughly one code per update plus one per like milestone.

### Inferences
- To get indexed: post every code in a public, pinned "codes" channel on Discord and on an X account the same minute the update ships, and name codes predictably (RELEASE, UPDATE1, 10KLIKES). Listing-site writers monitor exactly those sources; a DM to @PggRoblox or the Muck Rack-listed editors with the game link, code list and update cadence costs nothing and is how new games get a page.
- For a race-based isekai game, the ecosystem-standard rewards are race rerolls/spins, a small amount of premium currency, and XP/stat boosts; the first codes should be cheap (1–2 spins) so later milestone codes can escalate.
- Cadence seen in the field: one code per content update plus one per like milestone (e.g. 1K/5K/10K likes for a small game, scaled down from 100K/250K/400K for Anime Dice), and occasional event codes. Weekly updates therefore imply roughly one new code per week.
- Codes-site SEO is a durable traffic source only after the game has a page; expect the page to appear only once a site decides the game is "live" (has visible players/likes), so the week-1 burst and the first like milestone matter for being picked up.

### Gaps
- No listing site publishes an explicit minimum-popularity threshold for covering a game, nor a developer submission form; contact appears to be via editors/X. (Pro Game Guides, Try Hard Guides, Pocket Tactics and Pocket Gamer pages could not be fetched; statements come from search summaries.)
- No data on how many visits a codes page actually sends to a small game.

## Key Question 3: Launch mechanics — timing, like ratio, burst vs rollout under the 28-day window, Experience Events, paid vs free, Discord pings, how the big hits actually got traction

### Takeaway
Roblox's official discovery docs (updated 2026) score games on per-user averages — play-through rate, first-play bounce rate, play days per user, playtime per user (capped at 60 min/user/day), intentional co-play days, qualified play sessions, spend — across day 1, days 2–7 and days 8–28, and explicitly say that "even a small number of people playing" can get a game considered; so a launch burst is useful only as a retained-cohort generator. Community data points to US weekend afternoons (≈2 pm ET) and Friday ≈9 pm ET as peak-player windows, with June–July the seasonal peak. Of the big hits, most grew from an organic/creator-driven spike plus live-ops, not from paid ads: Grow a Garden was bought at ~1,000 CCU and scaled with weekly events; Dead Rails got Roblox's own TikTok and Flamingo; Fisch "skyrocketed unexpectedly"; Blox Fruits took ~18 months and a Christmas patch plus its own YouTube channel.

### Cited Findings

**How discovery treats a new game (official)**
- Two-stage system: retrieval (based on "engagement, retention, and monetization") then personalised ranking. Most important signals: play-through rate from Recommended for You, first-play bounce rate (measured at <60s and 61–180s), play days per user, playtime per user ("maximum of 60 minutes per user, per game, per day"); also important: intentional co-play days, qualified play sessions ("filters out accidental clicks"), spend days and Robux spent per user. "All signals use per-user averages, not totals", so small games "with engaged players aren't disadvantaged". Signals are measured over day 1, days 2–7 and days 8–28. "Games that have even a small number of people playing can signal to the system that the game is worth considering." Listed accelerators: sponsored ads, curation, search visibility, charts, friend referrals, notifications, social-media sharing. Visibility reducers: giveaway-focused metadata ("A game titled 'Robux! Play now!' receives less exposure"), metadata mismatched to gameplay, duplicate games. [official] — [discovery.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/discovery.md) (rendered at [create.roblox.com/docs/discovery](https://create.roblox.com/docs/discovery))
- 15 June 2026 "Optimizing Discovery" (John Ciancutti, Chief Growth Officer): Recommended For You moved from a 7-day to a 28-day evaluation window, with the day 1 / 2–7 / 8–28 buckets, to stop rewarding games "that win attention with exciting thumbnails but don't deliver long-term value"; headline signal "intentional co-play days"; platform at 132M DAU. ### Inferences
- Burst vs rollout: the official per-user, 28-day scoring means a 72-hour burst neither helps nor hurts by itself; what matters is the retention of the cohort it brings. The burst is still the right structure for a solo dev because (a) creator posts, Discord pings and an Experience Event can all be stacked in one window, (b) the day-1 bucket needs *some* qualified sessions to be scored at all ("even a small number of people playing"), and (c) a weekend-afternoon US launch maximises the chance that the first players bring friends (co-play signal). The counter-risk is a day-1 cohort that bounces (<60 s) — so the first-time-user flow, not the marketing, is the launch-critical item.
- Launch time to target: Saturday around 2 pm ET (= 9 pm Israel) or Friday around 9 pm ET (= 4 am Israel Saturday) per community data; a Saturday 2 pm ET launch is the most practical for an Israeli solo dev. Avoid the week before major school starts; June–July is the seasonal peak, which an October 2026 launch misses — Halloween week favours horror, which does not help an isekai title.
- Free, not paid access: official docs say paid access limits joins, blocks Xbox, and disables private servers; none of the recent hits launched paid. Paid access only makes sense for a tiny closed beta with the Discord community before the public launch.
- Use an Experience Event for the launch window even if 1,000 RSVPs (needed for the Trending Events chart) is out of reach; the RSVP notification and "Join Event" button are free and work at any size. Keep the 3-day minimum between update announcements in mind when planning the week-2 update.
- Budget line to add: verify whether the 1,000-Robux publishing fee / subscription requirement applies to your account before launch (official docs, 2026); if it does, it is refundable only after 60 days at 25+ "highly engaged" players.
- The hits' common pattern: a novel-but-familiar concept, a creator or official-channel spike within weeks 1–8, and immediate weekly live-ops. None started with large paid ad budgets; several were bought/scaled by studios *after* an organic signal at ~1,000 CCU. For a solo dev, the realistic goal of launch week is a retained core (hundreds, not thousands) plus clips that creators want to react to.

### Gaps
- No official or large-sample data on launch day-of-week; the hour-of-day numbers are a 2022 community resource and the mid-2026 CCU swing is an analyst estimate.
- No official statement on how a new experience is bootstrapped into Recommended For You in 2026 (cold start); only community reports of multi-day delays.
- No quantitative evidence that like ratio affects conversion or ranking; only the third-party 88–95% benchmark.
- Full growth narratives for Grow a Garden, Steal a Brainrot, Fisch and Dead Rails could not be fetched (Wikipedia/press domains blocked); figures above come from search summaries of those pages.

## Key Question 4: First 28 days — update cadence and title tags, like goals and event weekends, codes per update, community management, early dislikes, likes/dislikes visibility

### Takeaway
Official docs say description hashtags/tags are "unused in our ranking algorithms", endorse "[NEW]"-style markers only inside the description's Recent Updates section, and limit update announcements to 60 characters once per three days; multiple developers report search-rank drops after editing the title, and Community Standards prohibit clickbait/spam and rewarding players for likes. Likes/dislikes remain publicly visible in 2026 (a "Detailed Player Feedback with Ratings" test added written feedback in 2025 but did not replace the ratio); dislike-bombed games get little recourse beyond support tickets.

### Cited Findings

**Title / description / tags**
- Description guidance: 1,000-character limit; the first ~160-character "hook" should carry "primary search terms/keywords", "your title, core genre, and the most unique feature"; "Tags and hashtags are explicitly not recommended. They are unused in our ranking algorithms and serve only to eat up your character count limit"; emojis are fine "for extra flair"; an optional "Recent Updates" section should be "short, only highlighting the main attractions of your updates" with markers like "[NEW]" and seasonal tags. ### Inferences
- Keep the title fixed (name + genre keyword, e.g. "Name: Isekai Battlegrounds"); put "[NEW RACE] …" in the description's Recent Updates block, the thumbnail and the 60-char update announcement. Weekly title edits risk search-rank loss (community) and give no ranking benefit (official: tags unused). Rotating thumbnails is the safer "update signal".
- Weekly update cadence fits the official 3-day announcement limit and the 28-day scoring; plan updates for Friday evening / Saturday US so the days-2–7 and 8–28 buckets see return visits.
- Like goals: use "celebration" codes posted after a milestone is reached (ecosystem norm) rather than any per-player "like to claim" mechanic (prohibited). Never gate rewards on likes/favorites in-game; gate on group join or follow instead.
- Early dislikes: cannot be removed; the realistic defence is a high day-1 like rate from the Discord cohort (ask, do not reward) and a fast patch cadence. If bombed, document and email support/devrelations.
- Discord: since links are hidden from under-16s, do not rely on Discord as the primary funnel for a kid-heavy anime audience; use the Roblox community (group) page, Experience Events RSVPs, in-game follow prompts and experience notifications instead.

### Gaps
- No official statement on whether frequent title edits affect search or Recommended For You; only community anecdotes.
- No official definition of how like milestones interact with the "no rewards for likes" rule; the gap between the rule and widespread milestone codes is unresolved.

## Key Question 5: Risks — moderation pitfalls in marketing, anime IP copyright, scams targeting small developers

### Takeaway
The main marketing-side moderation risks are: rewarding likes/favorites, running raffles/lotteries or Robux prize promotions, sharing Discord/social links in-game, "free Robux" claims in promotional content, misleading thumbnails/titles, and "Blox"/"Roblox" in the name; the main IP risk is anime franchises (the 2023 Gamefam/Crunchyroll DMCA that privated Anime Adventures and Anime Fighting Simulator X, plus the Blox Piece and Saitama Game renames); the main scam risks are impersonators posing as creators/buyers/staff on Discord, credential-harvesting "partnership" offers, .rar "verification" files, and botted visits/likes/followers that trigger bans.

### Cited Findings

**Moderation pitfalls in marketing**
- Prizes/raffles: community rules quoted on DevForum say "you should not create promotions offering prizes of any sort (including contests, raffles, lotteries, chain letters or any kind of giveaway)", while an older DevForum thread says Robux giveaways are allowed "as long as they are not fraudulent" in "small quantities"; sources conflict. [community quoting rules, 2019–2021, older] — [DevForum "X likes for next reward"](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/do-tos-allow-x-likes-for-next-reward/1029127) vs [DevForum Giveaway Rules](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/help-giveaway-rules/342816); related [Discord invite competition with Robux prizes](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/discord-invite-competition-with-robux-as-prizes-allowed-or-not/1935716); [Are competitions for Robux allowed](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/are-competitions-for-robux-allowed/457013)
- Gambling: Roblox prohibits simulated and actual gambling and bars exchanging real money, Robux or items of value in connection with it. [official] — [Roblox Community Standards](https://about.roblox.com/community-standards)
- Discord giveaways are additionally governed by Discord's Master Sweepstakes Rules. [official, Discord] — [Discord](https://support.discord.com/hc/en-us/articles/16000885744791-Master-Sweepstakes-Rules)
- Advertising Standards: no "free Robux" enticements, mandatory "ad/paid/sponsored" disclosure, no promotion of M-rated/TV-MA media; developers "fully responsible". [official] — [Advertising Standards](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/13722260778260-Advertising-Standards); [DevForum 2026 standards](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/new-advertising-policies-standards/4527365)
- Detail-page content (thumbnail, title, description) must be All Ages or 9+ with enforcement from 2025. [official] — [DevForum experience guideline policies](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/updating-experience-guideline-policies-to-keep-our-younger-users-safe/3249079)
- Like-for-reward prohibited; in-game Discord links prohibited; social links 16+ only (see Q4). [official/community]
- Botted visits/followers: bot visits are "fake views pumped up by scripts"; Roblox "has systems in place to detect bot activity, and getting caught … can lead to your game being taken down, your account being suspended, or even permanently banned"; a 2025 "free follower bot" game was flagged as likely to get users banned; Roblox ran bot-account ban waves across selected games in 2026; place botting has also been used *against* developers to get games put under review. [community/press] — [bot-visit explainer (grey-market page, treat as hostile)](https://bot-roblox-game-visits.pages.dev/posts/bot-roblox-game-visits/); [RoNews on X](https://x.com/RoGameNews/status/1977069910380757208); [Bloxy News on ban wave, citing DevForum 4820656](https://www.threads.com/@bloxy.news/post/DcPTkaeD9Dj/); [DevForum place botting explanation](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/place-botting-explanation-ways-to-combat-it/343101)

**Anime IP / copyright**
- Official DMCA guidance: infringement occurs even if you "credited the original creator" or "downloaded the content from the internet"; on a valid claim "your game will be taken down or access to it will be disabled" and "Roblox will issue a warning on your account"; counter-notices via copyright_agent@roblox.com, restoration "within 10–14 business days" if no lawsuit; Roblox must terminate "repeat copyright infringers". [official] — [dmca-guidelines.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/publishing/dmca-guidelines.md); Roblox also offers a Rights Manager for IP holders — [rights-manager.md](https://github.com/Roblox/creator-docs/blob/main/content/en-us/production/publishing/rights-manager.md)
- December 2023: Anime Adventures (Gomu, ~2B visits since 2021), Anime Fighting Simulator X and Anime Fighting Simulator were privated after DMCA notices attributed to Gamefam/Crunchyroll over My Hero Academia assets, coinciding with the licensed "My Hero Academia: Battlegrounds". [press] — [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/news-roblox-enforces-dmca-anime-games-face-danger); [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/anime-adventures/private); [DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/roblox-anime-adventures-removed/); [RTC on X](https://x.com/Roblox_RTC/status/1738005940128100735)
- 2026: Anime Adventures' team announced a licensed spin-off, "TenSura: Isekai Adventures", and DMCA'd the unauthorised remake "Re:Adventures"; Anime Adventures itself remains private. [press] — [AllThings.How](https://allthings.how/anime-adventures-roblox-return-tensura-reveal-and-the-re-adventures-dmca/); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/all-roblox-anime-adventures-codes)
- Precedent renames: "Blox Piece" → "Blox Fruits" on 25 Dec 2019 because of copyright; "Saitama Game/Battlegrounds" → "The Strongest Battlegrounds" on 10 Apr 2023 due to copyright issues. [community wiki] — [Roblox Fandom Blox Fruits](https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Gamer_Robot_Inc/Blox_Fruits); [TSB Fandom](https://the-strongest-battlegrounds-rblx.fandom.com/wiki/Sorcerer)
- Name rule: "Roblox", "Blox" or similar are prohibited in experience names (post-22 June 2022). [official] — [Roblox Terms of Use](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004647846-Roblox-Terms-of-Use); [DevForum deletion case](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/game-deleted-for-having-roblox-in-the-title/4367418)
- Gamefam, a licensed-IP publisher, has partnered with Crunchyroll and is cited as the DMCA filer in 2023 — i.e. licensed competitors police anime IP on Roblox. [press/Wikipedia] — [Gamefam, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamefam)

**Scams targeting small developers**
- "Fake developer partnership offers": a message claiming to be from a known developer/studio offers Robux or limited items for "testing"; the link is a credential-harvesting page. [security news, 2026] — [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/news/roblox-scams-in-2026-what-players-are-actually-falling-for)
- "Advanced acquisition scam": a "buyer" on Discord offers to purchase your game/group, builds trust, then sends a .rar "verification" file containing a token grabber/backdoor to hijack the account and transfer ownership; documented repeatedly on DevForum. [community, 2025–2026] — [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advanced-acquisition-scam-targeting-roblox-game-and-group-owners/4156313); related losses: [scammed by two fake developers after a game transfer](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/i-was-scammed-by-two-fake-developers-after-a-game-transfer-%E2%80%93-a-warning-to-others/3859583); [group and 5 million Robux lost](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-i-was-scammed-out-of-my-group-and-5-million-robux/4724698)
- Impersonation of YouTubers/developers/staff on Discord and TikTok to push phishing links; a fake "Scriptbloxian Studios" Discord allegedly stole 100M+ Robux; a fake Caylus Discord targeted fans. [press/community] — [RbxScams, Discord impersonation](https://rbxscams.org/articles/impersonation-scams-fake-staff-targeting-roblox-players-on-discord); [RbxScams, TikTok impersonation of developers](https://rbxscams.org/articles/tiktok-impersonation-of-roblox-developers-and-staff); [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/929596-roblox-hackers-exposed-steal-robux-scam-fake-discord-scriptbloxian-studios)
- Roblox was the second-most impersonated brand in phishing in NordVPN's 2026 Consumer Cybersecurity Report (12.32% of cases). [industry report via press] — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/scammers-are-going-after-young-roblox-players-and-their-robux/)
- Grey-market sellers offer "ban/termination services" (mass false reports) against revenue-earning developers, and botting has been used to push games under review. [community] — [DevForum place botting](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/place-botting-explanation-ways-to-combat-it/343101)

### Inferences
- Creator-deal hygiene for a $80 budget: pay only through an escrow-style marketplace (Social Seed/Collabstr) or after the video is live; verify a creator's identity via a message from the channel's own "About"/community tab, never via a Discord DM; never open .rar/.exe "media kits"; never share a cookie/2FA code; use a separate Roblox account with no group ownership for outreach testing.
- Naming: avoid any real anime title, character name or "Blox" in the game name; keep the six races and cutscenes original (Type Soul and TSB survive by being "inspired by", not by using licensed names/assets). Register originals in Rights Manager only if you own trademarks; otherwise document originality (dated design docs) in case of false claims.
- Launch promotions: no raffles/lotteries and no Robux-prize giveaways tied to likes/invites; a "first 100 players get a cosmetic" code is the safe equivalent. Keep all "sponsored" labels on creator posts and never promise "free Robux" in a thumbnail, code name or title.
- Treat any sudden burst of visits or likes you did not buy as a possible attack and keep screenshots/analytics exports for a support ticket.

### Gaps
- Exact current Community Standards text on giveaways/raffles could not be fetched (help/about domains blocked); the rule is reconstructed from DevForum quotations that conflict and are 2019–2021 vintage.
- No 2025–2026 case of a DMCA against a Roblox game for *naming* alone (vs. assets) was found; the renames of Blox Piece and Saitama Game are community-reported as "copyright" without the notice being public.
- No documented 2026 case specifically of a fake "YouTuber" charging a small developer for a promo and not delivering; the closest documented patterns are acquisition and partnership scams.
